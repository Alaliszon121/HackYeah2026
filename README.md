# Bez RÓŻnicy.

### Same product. Different aisle. Different price. Totally normal, right?

**A 3D serious game about the Pink Tax, consumer awareness, and looking twice at the price tag.**

Built in Unity for HackYeah 2026 by **SO BAM!**

---

## What is Bez RÓŻnicy.?

You walk into a store. There are products, there are prices, and then there are... other prices.

**Bez RÓŻnicy.** is a 3D serious game that turns the issue of the **Pink Tax** into an interactive shopping experience. Enter a virtual store and find out how well you can estimate, compare, and organize the prices of everyday products.

Instead of simply presenting statistics, the game lets you experience the problem yourself. You make the decisions, compare the products, guess the prices, and sometimes discover that two things that look suspiciously similar do not have suspiciously similar price tags.

The goal is to increase awareness of price differences between products marketed toward different groups and encourage more conscious purchasing decisions. At the end of the game, you receive your score and learn more about the Pink Tax through published research and real-world price data collected by our team.

## The problem

Every day, stores present us with products marketed toward specific genders, especially in categories such as personal care, hygiene, clothing, accessories, and other everyday consumer goods.

Products marketed toward women and men may differ in packaging, branding, ingredients, size, features, positioning, and price. Price differences between products marketed toward women and comparable products marketed toward men are often described as the **Pink Tax**.

But the issue is more complicated than:

> “The pink one costs more.”

And that is exactly what makes it interesting.

Consumers may find it difficult to judge whether a higher price comes from a meaningful difference in the product itself or simply from how that product is marketed and positioned. The problem is therefore not only the price difference, but also how difficult that difference can be to **notice, compare, and evaluate** during normal shopping.

---

## The Pink Tax is not always obvious

Research shows that the phenomenon is measurable, but also more nuanced than it may first appear.

A large-scale study published in *Marketing Science*, based on sales data from **39,697 stores in the United States**, found that personal care products marketed toward women had, on average, a **10.6% higher unit price** than comparable products marketed toward men when products from the same manufacturers were analyzed across nine categories.

At the same time, the study found that this difference became almost negligible when products with comparable ingredients were compared.

So the interesting question is not simply:

> **Are products for women more expensive?**

It is:

> **Why are these two products priced differently, and are those differences actually justified?**

That distinction matters. **Bez RÓŻnicy.** is not meant to tell the player that every price difference is unfair. It is meant to make the player stop, compare, and ask better questions.

---

## Based on real-world shopping data

The products and prices used in **Bez RÓŻnicy.** are not fictional.

As part of the project, our team took a trip to the M2 shopping mall in Kraków and conducted our own price comparison of everyday products marketed toward women and products marketed toward a general audience.

We walked through stores, compared products directly on the shelves, documented their prices and characteristics, and used those observations as the basis for the products featured in the game. The products players encounter and the prices they compare are therefore based on **real products and real prices observed during our own research**.

We wanted the experience to resemble an actual shopping situation rather than an artificial example created only to demonstrate the concept. After all, the interesting question is not whether we can invent a price difference. It is whether you would notice one when it is already sitting on the shelf in front of you.

## Why a game?

You can put statistics into a presentation, put them into a report, or create another chart. Humanity already has plenty of charts.

Price differences, however, are something people encounter during ordinary shopping, often without consciously noticing them. Instead of only telling players about the problem, we wanted them to experience it.

We were also inspired by fast, immediately understandable guessing games such as [**Size It Up**](https://magnitudle.com/size-it-up). Its idea is wonderfully simple: make an estimate, commit to it, and immediately discover how close you were. There is almost no barrier between **“I wonder if I know this”** and **“let me try.”**

We wanted to capture that same energy: short rounds, simple interactions, and immediate feedback in a format that takes seconds to understand.

But instead of estimating the size of an object, **Bez RÓŻnicy.** asks you to estimate something you interact with constantly: **its price**.

That quick guessing-game format then becomes a way to introduce something more informational. Why did you expect that price? Why do two similar products cost different amounts? What actually separates them, and would you have noticed the difference during your normal shopping?

**Bez RÓŻnicy.** puts the player in front of the shelf and asks them to make the comparison themselves. The game is built around three simple ideas:

### Notice. Compare. Question.

Look at the products and their prices, think about what actually makes them different, and decide whether the difference in price makes sense.

Instead of telling the player what to think, we give them the products, prices, and choices.

Reality gets to do the awkward part.

## Gameplay

A playthrough consists of several different shopping challenges.

### Guess the Price

How much does this product actually cost?

Enter your estimate in złoty and grosze and see how close your intuition is to the real price. Turns out “I buy things all the time” is not necessarily a financial qualification.

---

### Spot the Difference

Two products, two prices, and one uncomfortable question:

**How big is the difference?**

Calculate the price gap and see how accurately you can compare the products. The challenge encourages players to pay attention not only to the final price, but also to the relationship between similar products.

---

### Sort the Products

Compare products and organize them according to the challenge. The player has to look beyond packaging and marketing and focus on the information that actually matters.

A revolutionary concept for a supermarket aisle.

---

## The Receipt of Truth

At the end of the shopping run, the player receives a summary of their performance. The game keeps track of the player's decisions and presents them as a final receipt containing:

- the player's answers,
- product and price information,
- their results,
- an overall score,
- and additional information about the Pink Tax.

The receipt is not just a score screen. It is the point where the player's assumptions meet the actual numbers.

Historically, humans have not always enjoyed that meeting.

---

## And then...

Finish your shopping run and you will reach a dedicated final sequence, where the game goes beyond the score itself and provides more context about the Pink Tax, the research behind the topic, and the real-world data used during development.

---

## What we want players to take away

**Bez RÓŻnicy.** is not designed to prove that every product marketed toward women is automatically overpriced. We want players to ask better questions.

*Is the product actually different? Is the quantity different? Is the composition different? Are the features different? Or are we paying more because of packaging, positioning, and marketing?*

The core problem is not only the price difference itself, but how easily those differences can disappear into the background of everyday shopping.

If a player finishes the game and becomes slightly more suspicious of two nearly identical products sitting on different sides of an aisle, we consider that progress.
